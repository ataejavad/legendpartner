# Site Structure — Private Relationship Management

*Supersedes the matchmaking-house architecture. Read alongside CREATIVE-DIRECTION.md.*

---

## 1. What changed, and why

The business was described as a **matchmaking agency**: the product was an introduction, and the
engagement effectively ended at the first dinner. The business is now **private relationship
management**: the product is the relationship itself, managed across its life.

That is not a rename. It changes who the client is, what is sold, and therefore what the site must
be.

| | Matchmaking house | Relationship management practice |
|---|---|---|
| Client | An unattached person | Anyone serious about a private relationship — attached or not |
| Sold | A search, ending in introductions | A retained practice across four areas |
| Ends | When a match is made | It does not; it is reviewed annually |
| Competitive frame | Elite matchmakers | Private wealth management, family office |
| The introduction | The whole business | One area of four, and rarely the decisive one |

**The governing analogy:** a family office does not find you an investment and leave. It manages
capital across decades. Legend manages the relationship life the same way.

## 2. The four practice areas

These are the spine of the new architecture. Everything else on the site refers to them.

| | Area | What it is | Retained by |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Assessment** | Establishing what a partnership must be for this particular life, before any name is considered | Everyone, first |
| II | **Search & Introduction** | Finding the person, verifying them, and bringing two people together with reasons on the table | Those seeking a partner |
| III | **Formation** | The first year — the postponed conversations, and merging two established lives | New relationships, ours or not |
| IV | **Continuity** | A standing retainer through the years and the harder decisions | Long-term members |

Areas III and IV are entirely new revenue and entirely new audience: **people already in a
relationship are now clients.** That single change is what makes this "management" rather than
matchmaking, and it roughly doubles the addressable client base without widening the market
downward.

## 3. Three ways members arrive

The site now speaks to three states rather than one. This appears on the home page, on
`practice.html`, and as the first question in the application.

1. **Not in a relationship** → Assessment, then Search & Introduction
2. **In one, and serious about it** → Formation or Continuity
3. **At a threshold** — marriage, relocation, blending families, an unspoken decision → wherever
   they actually are

**Primary navigation** — five items, unchanged in count, changed in meaning:

`Practice · Method · Mandates · Network · Journal` + `Member access` + **Private Consultation**

*Practice* replaced *Experience* as the first item because the four areas — not the process — are
now the thing a visitor must understand first. *Membership* became *Mandates* because a mandate is
a standing instruction to act on someone's behalf, which is what is actually being bought.

**Footer** carries four columns: brand · The Practice (deep links to the four areas) · The House ·
Enquiries.

## 6. Copy changes that carry the repositioning

- Brand line: *Private Relationship Advisory* → **Private Relationship Management**
- Hero: *A more considered way to meet* → **The one thing you never delegated**
- Contrast list, first pair: *Not more matches / Better matches* → **Not an introduction service /
  A practice**; last pair: *Not transactional / Personal* → **Not a transaction / A retainer**
- Application, new first question: **"What brings you to us"** with the three arrival states
- Application, stage 04 now reads *"If we are to search, who for?"* and tells attached members to
  leave it blank
- Present-situation options extended with *In a relationship* and *Married*; intent options with
  *Help with the relationship I am in* and *Counsel at a threshold or decision*

## 7. What deliberately did not change

The visual system, tone, discretion standard, membership tiers and price points, the network
philosophy, and the portal. The repositioning is a change of **what is being managed**, not of how
the house presents itself — and the quiet-luxury register suits an ongoing retainer better than it
suited a one-off search.

## 8. Open items this raises

1. **Fee structure needs revisiting.** The three tiers still read as search-shaped. Continuity is
   an annual retainer, which is a different commercial shape from a fixed-term search — the
   mandates page states an annual review, but the figures were set for the old model.
2. **Formation and Continuity need practitioners.** Areas III and IV imply people who can counsel
   couples, not only search for candidates. The About page describes an advisory bench; whether it
   can actually staff these areas is a real-world question, not a copy question.
3. **The therapy boundary** is stated once, on `practice.html` under Continuity: *"We are not
   therapists and do not present ourselves as such."* Depending on jurisdiction, this may need to
   appear in the engagement letter as well — take advice.
4. **SEO shifts** from *luxury matchmaking* toward *private relationship management*, which has
   almost no search volume today. The matchmaking terms still appear in titles for area II, but the
   Journal now carries more of the acquisition weight. Worth accepting deliberately rather than by
   accident.
